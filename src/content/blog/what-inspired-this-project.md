I've been writing all my life. It started off as a simple journaling habit to help untangle my thoughts, then grew into [short stories I'd write for English & Creative Writing classes](/blog/high-school-short-stories) and editorials printed in the school newspapers, but regressed back to journaling to help me cope with the growing pains of college life during engineering school with a handful of technical reports for labs and my senior capstone projects. Crazily enough, I even won the Technical Writing Award for [a feasibility study I wrote for my Written Communication for Engineers class](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1a56F7DvSxZfhaaWtsQOqHoUYNxzMrbPw/view?usp=sharing). Now that I'm a full-time engineer, I still journal pretty regularly, but wanted to lean into this a bit more because I miss project-based writing.

![Cloud the Burmese cat](/src/assets/golden_cloud.jpg)
![Ocean eyes](/src/assets/cloud_blue_eyes.jpg)

But writing, alone, didn't satisfy my itch to create. Ever since I got a phone, I've also been taking pictures to help me remember moments I cherished. Kansas sunsets in the backyard of my childhood home, candids of family, and the beloved family Burmese cat: Cloud (may she rest in peace) were my primary subjects that reminded me to slow down and be more present. 

![Thanksgiving 2022](/src/assets/thanksgiving2022.jpg)
![Uncle's restaurant](/src/assets/uncles_restaurant.jpg)

This gave me my eye for framing because the zoom on my phone cameras were so bad, it forced me to find the exact right place to stand for my photos. I also took a photography class in middle school, but it was too prescriptive to allow any creativity, and the cameras were very cheap. My husband got me a top-end Sony camera for my birthday a couple years ago, and I've been taking it with me on hikes, perfecting my photographic eye and making my pictures look more professional by the day.

Another thing I've always wanted to do is build my own website using my moderate coding experience without paying a subscription for a something like Squarespace. I'm still definitely more comfortable using Python than JavaScript, but building this website was a fun way to integrate my engineering skills into my creative outlet! Not sure where any of this will take me, but I'm sticking to it for the sake of creating something meaningful to me: the stories behind my favorite photos.
