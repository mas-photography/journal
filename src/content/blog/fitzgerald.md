F. Scott Fitzgerald's voice, Creative Writing 2018 

In my younger and more agreeable years, my mother gave me some advice that I really wish would work.
	
‘Whenever you feel scared,’ she told me after I cried to her about my nightmares, ‘pinch yourself so that when you wake up, you’d know it was just a dream and when you don’t, you’d know that it’s real.’ 
	
I’ve pinched myself every night since then, and it’s never worked. Rather than releasing me from my dreams, my mind ties me to a chair then sets it on fire – the harder I try to leave, the worse my dreams become. I know these dreams aren’t real because I eventually wake up from them, but each moment feels as vivid as reality itself. Raw fear chills my throat as I run away from my own mind and the flavor of terror lingers on my tongue when I wake up at midnight with my heart pounding in my chest and my stomach heaving, threatening to vomit my last meal just to get rid of the taste. I seem to have no control, yet I know I control it all because they’re my dreams – figments of my own imagination. If I don’t control them, who does? 
	
These dreams have been trying to tell me something, but I don’t know what it is. If I control my own dreams, why can’t I understand what they’re trying to say? They’ve dragged me to different lairs and forced me to speak their native tongues. They’ve created this ambiguous puzzle that I can’t piece together and it’s driving me insane. They’ve shown me flashbacks and visions all mushed together into one continuous nightmare. They’ve muttered at me during school and screamed at me while I slept. I can’t stay in the present without thinking of the past and the future and what my dreams could be trying to say. This riddle has been strangling my brain and it won’t let go until I solve it. I pinch myself, but the dream continues.
