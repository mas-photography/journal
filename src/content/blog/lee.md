Harper E. Lee's voice, Creative Writing 2018 

When I was thirteen, I had irksome dreams that made me detest the very thought of sleeping. I drank coffee and tried to stay awake for days on end, but I’d eventually crash. When I realized caffeine only intensified my overactive imagination, I stopped. And looking back on it now, I’m glad I did. 

Even without caffeine, my dreams would, quite literally, speak to me. They frustrated me because they would always be so vague, and whenever I’d tell anyone about this, they’d say I’m crazy. My doctor, to no surprise, declared that I was depressed and in desperate need of medication.

He spoke over me to tell my mother which pills he wanted me to take and when. Then after he finished, I proceeded to tell him which utensils I wanted him to use and how. He wasn’t amused and I didn’t care. But my mother, to my dismay, still bought the overpriced medicine. And I, for some reason, still had to take them. 

For the record that my mother will inevitably find: I swallowed each pill in that bottle, just as Dr. Hett told me to. But off the record: I sold them all to my best friend’s brother. And it was the easiest $300 I have ever made, to this day. I despise medicine, and especially can’t see the appeal in using it un-prescribed by a competent doctor, so doing this took absolutely no skin off my back, and Jack seemed to really enjoy it. He’d always have this blissfully blank look in his eyes and then he’d take a nap. So to pay back my mother for this unexpected wealth, I bought her a bunch of jewelry over the span of five years.  

I still had excessive amounts of trouble falling asleep, but I realized then that no one could help me, so I stopped expecting them to. I was all alone, and thankfully, my dreams started making some sort of sense. My newly overcome caffeine addiction must have sparked some cohesion into my neurons. I started seeing patterns in my dreams and figured out that someone was trying to kill me. I avoided that and also managed to go back in time to prevent the Holocaust. It was pretty wild, and I’m still exhausted because these dreams won’t let me sleep.
