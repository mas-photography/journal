Tell-Tale Orphan, English at BCCC 2019

Mia sat leaned back in her seat as she sipped a glass of water. Her eyes lingered on the now crusted bloodstain in the carpet. The police surrounded the orphanage with caution tape and offered her their consolidation in the form of a blanket she did not take. It was almost midnight, but Mia was wide awake.  

She could still hear the voices in her head -- the ones that prompted her to murder her
childhood friend a few years back, but she didn’t mind. She knew they would lead her out of this orphanage, and that was all that mattered for the time being. 
Don’t get the wrong idea, now. Mia wasn’t insane or anything; she just needed a way out. You see, she’s lived in this hell-hole for the past ten years and her life has only gotten worse. She wouldn’t want me to bore you with the details, but she’d be happy to let you know that she’s finally found her way out. It wasn’t, by any means, legal (or moral, for that matter). It simply was just as I stated it: a way out. 
	
You might be wondering who I am, but that doesn’t matter. Just as any respectable narrator would, I implore you to focus on my story and not on me. 
	
Now, back to the story: what was her way out? Murder. The police have surrounded the orphanage because the owner has somehow been murdered, but little do they know: it was Mia, the sweetest-looking orphan there, who did it and she didn’t plan on stopping. 
	
Mia took a few shaky breaths before adopting a timid, meek persona as she stumbled over to the officer to ask him if he’s found out who the murderer was, and I almost believed her! Mia has always been a fantastic actress -- the star of every school play -- no one could ever tell when she was lying. No one but me. When she stepped behind him and snapped his neck, the officer didn’t suspect a thing. 
	
Mia grabbed a particularly jagged bread knife from the kitchen and made her way to the dormitory. She glanced out the window and took in the view of the grassy plains as she climbed the steps two at a time. For the past ten years, no one even offered to give Mia someplace to call home and it’s turned her slightly bitter. Mia didn’t want to kill her fellow orphans -- she had to. She opened the door to the dormitory and didn’t even try to stay quiet as she locked it behind her. 
	 
Mia walked by each bed and slit all the girls’ throats without skipping a beat. Hearing the screams from the girls she hadn’t yet killed did nothing to stop her. I knew this type of thing wouldn’t faze someone as strong-willed as her, no matter how loudly each girl screamed. She was such a natural -- best I’ve ever seen, in fact. 

I unlocked the door and held it open for her, then didn’t even bother to shut it behind us. I took a bottle of kerosene from my pocket and spread it throughout the house before I lit a match and dropped it on the doormat on our way out. 
	
“This,” I said, gesturing to the burning house, “marks the end of your old life and start of your new one. How do you feel?” Mia gazed into the flames then nodded before saying, “Good.” And with that, we walked to the MI6 headquarters to start her training. 

I know that you’re probably repulsed or terrified of what you’ve just read, but keep in mind that no one is ever completely good and even the worst people can’t be completely horrible. Besides, this was the first time Mia’s smiled in months. You wouldn’t want to ruin that, would you?
