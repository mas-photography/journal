Mary Shelley's voice, Creative Writing 2018 

New York City, Feb. 11th, 2018

TO Dr. Hett 

As I’m sure you know, you are the only therapist in the whole state of New York who hasn’t refused to see me. While I’m well aware that I have become a joke among you and your peers, I implore you to keep reading because I’m afraid you are my only hope. 

For the past three years, I’ve been having these traumatizing dreams of historic events with me in them, and they all seem to connect somehow. I can’t figure out why or even what they mean. But I feel like each dream I’ve had has been trying to tell me something, trying to show me something. As I learn more about these events in my history class, my dreams become even stranger. I’ve tried to stay unbothered by them, but they won’t let me, they always come back to haunt me; and when they do, it’s as if they steal my own mind from me. Just yesterday, when I was walking from history to math, I started seeing the scenes from my dreams in place of the hallways surrounding me. After I sat in my desk, my dream completely overtook me – SS officers dragged me to my agonizing death; while the students around me only saw a glazed, indifferent expression on my face, I was being burned alive. And it wasn’t even the first time that had happened to me. For the past three years, I’ve been crying for help, telling everyone who would listen about my horrible dreams, but no one ever believed me. And I don’t blame them either: if I weren’t the one experiencing these dreams, I wouldn’t believe myself, and even now, when I know these awful dreams are as real as can be, I don’t want to believe it. I don’t want to, but I do because they feel as real as reality itself; how can they not be real and still be keeping me up at night? Because of these dreams, I’ve died three times in the past week. Last Tuesday, I tasted the ashes of Pompeii as my home collapsed onto my family; last Thursday, I felt the dust particles replace the air in my lungs as a vicious dust storm stripped away the walls of my mother’s tiny, wooden house; and yesterday, I felt my throat scorch and shrivel as the crackling crematorium fire sucked all the moisture out of my body. 

My dreams are trying to tell me something, and they won’t leave me alone until I understand what they say; they won’t let me sleep, they won’t let me wake, they won’t even let me go to school in peace. I don’t know what they want from me. I’ve been trying to figure it out for the past three years: I’ve been writing down all my dreams in excruciating detail, and trying to piece together this ambiguous puzzle, but I can’t. I can’t do this by myself because it’s driving me insane. I need your help. You’re the only doctor in New York who will listen to me, so please; help me save myself from my dreams.
